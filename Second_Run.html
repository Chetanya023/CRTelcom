        Questions we hear first.
        Plain answers on the rules that trip up most first launches.
        
    
        
      
        What is DLT and do I need it? 
        DLT is the TRAI-mandated registry for commercial SMS in India. You register your business (entity), your sender headers and every message template on an operator-run DLT portal. Messages that don't match a registered header and template are blocked.
        If you plan to send SMS, it's the first thing to start because approvals take time.
        
      
        Do WhatsApp and RCS need DLT? 
        WhatsApp messages follow Meta's rules: a verified business, an approved template for business-initiated messages and customer opt-in. RCS follows Google and carrier verification. Regulators and operators are still aligning on traceability for RCS, so we check current requirements with you before launch.
        
      
        SMS, RCS or WhatsApp: which should I use? 
        Use SMS for anything that must reach every phone, like OTPs. Use WhatsApp where customers already chat and you want two-way conversations. Use RCS for rich, branded messages on supported Android devices. Most teams combine them and let fallback handle the rest.
        
      
        What are 140, 1600 and 1800 numbers? 
        The 140 series is for promotional and telemarketing calls. The 1600 series is for service and transactional calls, and TRAI has directed banks, insurers and similar regulated entities to move those calls onto it. 1800 numbers are toll-free numbers for inbound calls. We help match your use case to the right series.
        
      
        How long does onboarding take? 
        It depends on the channel. SMS registrations and template approvals typically take days. WhatsApp verification and template approval can take days to a couple of weeks. RCS brand and carrier approvals can take several weeks. We run them in parallel so the slowest channel doesn't hold the others back.
        
      
        Can I use my own sender name and WhatsApp number? 
        Yes. Sender headers are registered under your business on DLT, and your WhatsApp number is verified under your own Meta business account. A number that is already active on the WhatsApp app needs to be moved to the Business Platform first, and we'll walk you through that.
        
      
        What happens when a message can't be delivered? 
        You get a failed event with a reason through your webhook. If you've set a fallback order, such as WhatsApp then RCS then SMS, the next channel is tried automatically.
